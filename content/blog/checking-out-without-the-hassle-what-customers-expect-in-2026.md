Jef fills his basket, taps checkout and is then faced with a form of twelve fields and one payment method he never uses. He closes the tab. You have lost him in the very last metre.

That is the nasty thing about selling online: the hardest work, convincing the customer, is done, and then the checkout throws it away.

> A form with twelve fields is not a checkout. It is an obstacle course.

## Where customers drop off

Almost every webshop knows the same five reasons why a customer stops halfway:

- **Surprise costs.** Shipping or extras that only appear in the last step.
- **A mandatory account.** Someone who wants to buy something does not want a new relationship.
- **Too many fields.** Every extra field is a reason to hesitate.
- **The payment method is missing.** Anyone who does not see their usual method leaves.
- **An unclear error message.** 'Something went wrong' helps nobody.

## What customers expect

- **The method they use every day.** In Belgium that means Bancontact, cards and payment apps on the phone.
- **As few fields as possible.** Name, address, payment. Done.
- **The total before the last click.** Including shipping and VAT, with no surprises.
- **An immediate confirmation,** by email.
- **A clear way out when something goes wrong.** An email address or a button, not a maze.

## Why payment integration is more than a button

A payment is rarely the end. It tells your order that the money has arrived, it prepares an invoice and it helps your bookkeeping. That is why we do not build payment as a loose button, but as a link in a chain:

**Order** → **Payment** → **Confirmation** → **Invoice** → **Bookkeeping**

Each link tells the next one what has happened.

## What happens behind the scenes

When a customer pays, a lot happens that they never see. The payment provider confirms the payment with a signal to your website. Your website checks that signal and updates the order. The rest follows: the email, the invoice, the stock.

If that signal does not arrive, or arrives twice, you end up with empty orders or duplicate invoices. That is why we build this with checks at every step and with retries for when something fails.

## Refunds and disputes

A refund should be a small action, not a project. Keep your records in good order: who, what and when. When a customer disputes a payment, a tidy administration is what wins it.

## Paying safely

- **Strong customer authentication.** In the EU it is required for most online card payments (the PSD2 rules). Choose a provider that handles it.
- **Do not store card details yourself.** Leave that to the provider.
- **Always use a secure connection (https)** across your whole site.
- **Choose a provider within the EU,** and put agreements about data in writing.

## The payment world is shifting

Payment methods change faster than websites. Europe is working on its own options, such as Wero, which are meant to replace existing apps and systems over time. The details and dates keep moving, so check what your payment provider supports now and later. Ideally, choose one that adds new methods quickly.

## Checkout check for your webshop

- Can the customer pay with the method they choose themselves?
- Do they see the total before the last click?
- Does your form have fewer than seven fields?
- Do they get a confirmation by email straight away?
- Can you refund an amount in one click?
- Do the money and the order land in your administration straight away?

*Jef does not exist. His closed tab does, somewhere.*
